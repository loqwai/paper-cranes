# lattice-bead/6.frag — timbral colour

**Forked from** `4.frag` on **2026-09-04**, on branch `lab/react-colour`.

4.frag stays the legible bead. It won **recognition** — figure/ground separation via `LEGIBLE`, at
`knob_169=0.60 navZoom=0.14 legible=1 knob_168=1.0` — and **5 does not touch one line of geometry**.
Everything below is in the colour channel.

The brief was: make the colour react to **timbre**, so the field evolves on spectral character
rather than loudness. The interesting part turned out to be that the palette had a structural bug
underneath it, and the feature and the fix are the same edit.

---

## 1. The bug 4.frag could not see

`lush()`'s chroma was

```glsl
C = (cb + seed2*cs) + ca * sin(s * TAU * 0.5 + 1.3)
```

Hue is `fract(s)` — **period 1**. That chroma term has **period 2**. Measured on a JS port of the
exact function (theme 0, `seed2 = 0.755`), before a line of the shader changed:

| s | hue | C | | s | hue | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.000 | 0° | **0.2109** | | 1.000 | 0° | **0.1146** |
| 0.125 | 45° | **0.2124** | | 1.125 | 45° | **0.1131** |
| 0.250 | 90° | 0.2063 | | 1.250 | 90° | 0.1192 |
| 0.500 | 180° | 0.1761 | | 1.500 | 180° | 0.1494 |

**The same hue renders at 1.84× the chroma depending only on where the unbounded accumulators
(`bTime`, `paletteShift`, `sectionMode`) happen to sit.** Two consequences, both shipped:

- **Saturation was a slow oscillator nobody wired.** Over a set `paletteShift` climbs
  monotonically, so the whole palette silently fades to pastel and back on a **2.0 cycle of
  `paletteShift`**. That is what "fuzzy terrible fuchsia" is: not a bad hue, a hue caught at
  C 0.113 with nothing in the shader that knows it happened or can recover it.
- **Per-material colour was inexpressible.** `lush(s + 0.33, 1.0)` for the rim changed the rim's
  *chroma* as an unavoidable side effect of changing its *hue*. "Same colour family, hotter edge"
  was not a thing the function could say — which is exactly what the brief asked for.

## 2. What 6.frag does

Chroma becomes an **explicit argument** (`lushC`) and is driven by **timbre** instead of by hue
position. Four moves, all in colour.

### 2a. TONALITY → chroma

```glsl
#define TONALITY (clamp((spectralCrestSmooth - 0.45) * 1.9 - (spectralEntropyMedian - 0.78) * 1.8, -1.0, 1.0))
```

Clean/peaky spectrum = vivid, noisy/flat = grey. Both halves are slow by construction — one is the
controller's smoothed crest, the other a ~500-frame median — and both are listed **independent of
energy**, so saturation tracks what the sound *is* rather than how loud it is. In Oklch, moving C
at fixed L barely moves luminance, which is the only reason timbre is allowed near it at all: the
same wire into **lightness** would be a brightness pump and is forbidden.

**The centre mattered more than the gain.** The first cut centred entropy at 0.55, which is *below*
where music sits (`spectralEntropyMedian` runs ~0.75–0.85 and the harness pins 0.80). Ordinary
music therefore read as "noisy" and the frame came out **desaturated — sat 0.213 vs 4.frag's 0.322
on theme 3**, i.e. the change made the wash-out *worse*. Re-centred, typical music sits at
TONALITY ≈ 0 and timbre can swing both ways.

### 2b. Two hue fields at incommensurate rates

```glsl
float tPhaseA(){ return (iTime/3.0) * 0.00090  + (spectralSkewMedian     - 0.5) * 0.45; }
float tPhaseB(){ return (iTime/3.0) * 0.000556 + (spectralKurtosisMedian - 0.5) * 0.38; }
```

One turn of field A every **55.6 min**, field B every **90.0 min**, ratio **1.6187** (golden). Their
interference has no short common period, so the colour field keeps arriving somewhere it has not
been — with no accumulator that can saturate or reset. Each phase is offset (not *rated*) by one
slow SHAPE-domain median, so a timbre change re-lands the whole pattern somewhere else rather than
making the hue run fast. The hue can never be accelerated by the music, only relocated.

**Rate budget, computed rather than eyeballed** — this is the thing that gets shaders rejected:

```
|d hue/dt| = Σ A · TAU · (rate/3) · freqMultiplier
  field A: 0.088 · TAU · 0.00030   · (1 + 1.309) = 0.000383 turns/s
  field B: 0.064 · TAU · 0.00018533 · (1 + 0.764) = 0.000131 turns/s
  total                                          = 0.000514 turns/s = 0.0308 turns/min
```

The `bTime` hue term 4.frag already ships runs at **0.04 turns/min**, and the user rejected
**0.24** as "the palette flashing". So this is *slower than what is already there* and 8× under the
known failure.

### 2c. Material, not tint

Timbre sets how far **apart** ground / body / rim sit, not what colour the frame is. A tint moves
everything together and you stop seeing it; a material split changes the *relationship* between
figure and ground and the eye keeps noticing.

| lane | driver | domain | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| ground hue | `spectralRoughnessSmooth` (GRAIN) | quality | dissonance pushes ground off the bead |
| rim hue | `spectralSpreadMedian` (WIDTH) | shape | a wide spectrum throws the contour further round |
| all chroma | crest vs entropy (TONALITY) | quality | tonal = glazed, noisy = matte |
| field phases | skew / kurtosis medians | shape | where the interference sits |

Plus `pitchClassMean` (tonal) still carried from 4.frag. **Four different domains**, so this is not
one signal drawn four times.

A dissonant, wide-spectrum passage pushes the ground away from the bead and the frame reads
**grainy**; a clean narrow one collapses them and it reads **glazed**. Hue offsets are ≤ 0.26 turns
and median-driven, so the split breathes over tens of seconds — it does not track beats.

### 2d. Theme 4 "timbral"

Themes 0–3 are lifted verbatim from other lattice shaders and are left **byte-identical**. Theme 3
measurably washes out (L slope 1.20 pins L at the 0.92 ceiling for any `lit > 0.683` — most of a
lit bead is flat white), so 5 **adds a fifth entry** rather than editing a shared one: ceiling 0.80,
gentle slope, `ca` widened to 0.070 so TONALITY has real travel.

## 3. Measurements

All from `scripts/timbral-colour.mjs` (contact sheet + gamut + jitter), `scripts/gamut-quick.mjs`
(fast tuning probe) and `scripts/hue-isolate.mjs` (hue vs light isolation). Pinned features, headless
Chromium, `time=8`, mon = kiku, `legible=1 knob_169=0.60 navZoom=0.14`, 320×320 sample.
`?flatcolour=1` renders 4.frag's colour on the identical frame, so every row is a true A/B.

### Gamut

Two figures, because they measure different things. **`any0`** is the raw "a channel sits at 0"
count — the figure the old "11.93% of pixels crushed" note used. **CRUSH** is that count restricted
to pixels that are *not merely dark* (`max channel > 40`). A channel rounding to 0 inside a
near-black pixel is 8-bit quantisation, not a gamut failure, and counting it penalises any darker
palette for being darker. **I tuned against the raw figure for two rounds before noticing this and
made two changes for no reason** (see §4).

| palette | 6.frag any0 | 6.frag CRUSH | 4.frag any0 | 4.frag CRUSH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timbral (theme 4) | 9.54% | **2.45%** | 23.18% | 6.00% |
| Jade (0 / 1.35) | 4.31% | **1.43%** | 17.76% | 6.70% |
| Deep Cyan (1 / 0.45) | 4.22% | **0.81%** | 2.53% | 0.00% |
| Ember (1 / 1.05) | 1.34% | **0.00%** | 16.16% | 3.87% |
| Violet (1 / 0.75) | 0.04% | **0.00%** | 0.09% | 0.00% |
| **Acid Lime (0 / 0.15)** | 13.80% | **5.00%** | 0.00% | **0.00%** |
| Shipped default (0 / 1.7) | 0.00% | **0.00%** | 1.43% | 0.56% |
| Contrast (theme 3) | 0.00% | **0.00%** | 0.00% | 0.00% |
| **mean** | | **1.21%** | | **2.14%** |
| **worst** | | **5.00%** | | **6.70%** |

**Channels pinned at 255: 0.00% everywhere, both builds.** Nothing blows out.

Net: **1.8× less mean crush and 1.3× better worst case**, and the two palettes that were worst in
4.frag (Jade 6.70%, Timbral 6.00%) more than halve. The exception is a genuine regression, below.

### Hue jitter

Whole-frame, saturation-weighted circular mean hue, with the **fast** features re-rolled every
frame for 90 frames:

| build | mean | p95 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.frag timbral | **0.00122** turns/frame | 0.00236 | 0.03152 |
| 4.frag colour | 0.00146 turns/frame | 0.00292 | 0.03392 |

6.frag is **slightly lower** than the build it forks (within noise either way). The important part
is that it adds nothing.

That number is *not* hue jitter though, and the first version of this note nearly claimed it was.
Both builds reported the same figure, which is the giveaway: re-rolling fast features moves the
**lighting**, the lighting decides which pixels dominate a saturation-weighted mean, and the mean
wobbles even if every pixel's hue is frozen. `scripts/hue-isolate.mjs` therefore measures per-pixel
hue on a **fixed 64×64 grid** under four conditions — static / fast-only / slow-only / both — so the
light channel cannot drag the result. See §5 for the reading.

### Code audit

Every symbol entering the hue journey and the material lanes, classified:

- **Medians:** `pitchClassMean`, `spectralSkewMedian`, `spectralKurtosisMedian`,
  `spectralSpreadMedian`, `spectralEntropyMedian`
- **Controller smooth / spring / flow:** `melodyFlow`, `waveletCentroidSpring`, `bassNoteFlow`,
  `spectralCrestSmooth`, `spectralRoughnessSmooth`
- **Monotonic or discrete:** `sectionMode`, `sectionMix`, `paletteShift`, `bTime`, `iTime`
- **Hand / per-device:** `knob_2`, `seed`, `quietGate`, `world` (navigation)
- **Geometry:** `field`

**Zero raw features. Zero zScores. Zero energy-correlated terms.** Nothing is summed into
brightness or alpha; the global multiplier is untouched; frame feedback decay is unchanged at
`mix(prev * 0.90, col, 0.82)`.

### Contact sheet

`journals/lab/shots/timbral-colour.png` — 24 tiles (4 mon × 6 palettes) plus a 6-tile A/B of
6.frag vs 4.frag colour on identical frames. Raw numbers in
`journals/lab/shots/timbral-colour.json`.

**All four mon stay individually nameable in all six palettes.** The recognition read 4.frag bought
survives — helped, if anything, because the rim now carries the most chroma of the three materials
and the ground the least, so the drawn edge is the most saturated thing in frame.

The A/B on the shipped default (`theme=0 paletteShift=1.7`) is the clearest single result: 4.frag
renders the washed pink-lilac, 6.frag renders a coral bead on a lime ground at the same lightness
(lum 77.6 vs 78.8) and **0.00% crush in both**. Same frame, same geometry, same light — the entire
difference is that chroma stopped being a function of hue.

## 4. What failed — negative results

- **I tuned against the wrong metric for two rounds.** Counting every channel-at-0 pixel made
  darker palettes look worse for being darker. On that signal I trimmed the hue-field amplitudes
  (0.090 → 0.075) and cut theme 4's chroma (`cb` 0.140 → 0.118, `ca` 0.065 → 0.050). Once the metric
  excluded near-black pixels, the "worst" palette went from 8.95% to **2.4%** and *the richer
  setting measured better than the trimmed one* (worst 2.27% vs 2.40%). Both changes were reverted.
  A metric that moves with something you are not testing will send you the wrong way with total
  confidence.
- **Material chroma in `ca` units did nothing.** The first design expressed per-material chroma in
  the same `ca`-scaled units as the term it replaced — elegant, and invisible: `ca` is only
  0.04–0.07, so a 0.26 spread between rim and ground came to **0.013 of actual chroma**. Measured,
  moving those biases by 0.1 changed frame saturation by 0.003 while costing 0.6pt of crush. The
  envelope stays `ca`-scaled (so it matches 4.frag's range exactly) but the **material split had to
  become absolute** (`MATC_RIM = 0.032`) to be seen at all.
- **Acid Lime regressed, 0.00% → 5.00% crush.** The only palette that got worse. Cause: the
  interference field rotates regions onto hues that do not fit at that palette's high lightness
  (lum 86). It is the mirror of the win on Ember (3.87% → 0.00%) — the same rotation that moves
  regions *off* clipping hues on five palettes moves them *onto* one here. The damage is bounded:
  `fitGamut` compresses toward the neutral of the same lightness, so out-of-gamut pixels lose local
  saturation rather than snapping hue, which is the posterized failure mode a raw `clamp()` causes.
  Not fixed. If it matters live, the lever is `MATC_RIM` and the field amplitudes.
- **Theme 3 is still washed out.** Its L slope of 1.20 pins lightness at the ceiling for most of a
  lit bead, and *no colour change can fix a lightness problem*. 6.frag's theme 3 measures sat 0.220
  vs 4.frag's 0.322 — it is if anything whiter, because the hues it now lands on are less
  RGB-chromatic at that lightness. Theme 4 exists as the answer instead. Theme 3 was deliberately
  left byte-identical rather than "fixed", since it is shared verbatim with other lattice shaders.
- **Frame saturation is ~6% below 4.frag on average** (0.309 vs 0.328, RGB max−min). Chased this for
  two rounds before establishing it is **hue-driven, not chroma-driven**: the metric is confounded
  because green-dominant hues read far more RGB-chromatic than blue-dominant ones at equal Oklch C,
  and 6.frag's rotation lands more area on blue in this particular pinned frame. Raising chroma
  biases moved it by 0.003 and cost gamut. Left alone.
- **Not confirmed by a human.** Everything here is measured or rendered headless. Whether the
  material split *reads* as material — and whether the per-pixel hue movement in §5 reads as
  shimmer or as noise — needs eyes on the rig with real audio.

## 5. Hue vs light, isolated — and the one thing to watch live

Per-pixel hue on a fixed 64×64 grid, mean |Δhue| per frame in turns
(`scripts/hue-isolate.mjs`, 80 frames per cell):

| condition | 6.frag | 4.frag colour | minus noise floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STATIC** — nothing driven (probe noise floor) | 0.00094 | 0.00066 | — |
| **FAST** — fast features re-rolled, slow pinned | 0.00961 | 0.00692 | 0.00867 vs 0.00626 (**+39%**) |
| **SLOW** — fast pinned, medians drifting at max rate | 0.00139 | 0.00087 | 0.00045 vs 0.00021 (**+114%**) |
| **BOTH** | 0.01052 | 0.00671 | — |

Read it as two separate facts.

**SLOW is the feature.** The colour channel now moves about **2.1× more** with slow timbral medians
than 4.frag's did — which is the entire point, since in 4.frag timbre moved colour essentially not
at all. The absolute number is tiny (0.00045 turns/frame) and it is a **conservative upper bound**:
the harness drifts medians at ±0.002/frame, far faster than a real ~500-frame median can move.

**FAST is the cost, and it is in the light lane.** Fast audio moves per-pixel hue ~39% more than in
4.frag. This is *not* the hue field moving — that is audited clean in §3, and the fast features
never reach it. It is that the three materials now sit **further apart on the wheel**, so when the
light channel changes which lane dominates a pixel, the hue step across that boundary is bigger.
Note 4.frag is not at zero here either (0.00626): additive coloured lighting has always moved
per-pixel hue, because `col += lush(s + 0.33, …) * rim * (… trebLive …)` mixes a different hue in
as the rim brightens.

The whole-frame palette measure — the thing the user actually complained about — is **unchanged**
(§3, 0.00122 vs 0.00146). But per-pixel movement is what would read as **edge shimmer** on the rig,
and that is the one thing a measurement cannot settle. If it shows up, the levers are the rim hue
offset (`sRim`'s `0.26` coefficient) and `MATC_RIM`; a further option is to give the transient
drop-flare a hue closer to `sBody` so the per-beat flash re-tints less than the steady rim does.
