# lattice-bead/7.frag — the ChromaDepth bead

**Forked from** `4.frag` on **2026-09-04**. 4.frag stays the Oklch legible bead; `?cdoff=1` here
reproduces it exactly, same frame, for A/B.

Built to be viewed through **ChromaDepth 3D glasses**, where the lens is a prism and hue *is*
parallax — red bends least and lands nearest, violet bends most and lands furthest.

## Why this fork is small

4.frag had just solved figure/ground, and **a solved figure/ground is a depth structure**. It only
needed to be spoken in the language the glasses read:

| what | depth | hue | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| bead interior | 0.00 – 0.17 | 0.00 – 0.13 | **red → orange, NEAR** |
| contour rim | 0.34 – 0.47 | 0.26 – 0.35 | yellow-green → green, MID |
| lattice lines | 0.56 – 0.76 | 0.42 – 0.57 | green-cyan → blue |
| ground between beads | 0.82 – 0.97 | 0.62 – 0.73 | blue → **violet, FAR** |

The geometry, the audio mappings, the relief, the rim light, the specular, the aurora and the onset
flare are **untouched**. What changed is what they are allowed to *say*.

## The one structural idea

**Hue is spent entirely on depth, and everything else becomes exposure.**

In 4.frag every light source spoke in its own hue and the frame's colour was their sum. Summing
hues is exactly what a ChromaDepth lens reads as a third, wrong depth — the "green mush" failure in
`docs/advanced-shader-techniques.md` §3. So 7.frag runs 4.frag's entire pipeline unchanged, collapses
it to **one scalar** (a per-pixel exposure), and repaints at the depth's hue.

Nothing about the music is lost. It all arrives as light — which is where the standing channel
hierarchy wanted it anyway: *light takes all the audio, colour follows the slowest thing there is.*
Here the slowest thing is geometry itself.

Three consequences fall out of that decision, each of which had to be handled explicitly:

- **Exposure is the MEAN of channels, not luminance.** Luminance is hue-weighted (blue 0.07, green
  0.72), so with `paletteShift` permanently rotating `lush()`'s hue over a set, a luminance read
  would make the frame drift brighter and darker for reasons that have nothing to do with the music.
- **Frame feedback runs in the exposure domain only.** Mixing the previous frame's *RGB* would drag
  last frame's hue into this pixel — i.e. paint it at a depth it is not at — and a moving camera
  would leave a trail of wrong depths behind every bead. Only its HSL *lightness* is read back. The
  0.90 decay is preserved.
- **The vignette stays in RGB**, because scaling toward black is a uniform scale of the triple and
  therefore hue- and saturation-safe.

## The depth axis: `gSD`, and the three candidates that failed

The depth axis is `gSD` — the bead's own signed distance to its silhouette, normalised by the cell
radius (negative inside, 0 on the edge, positive outside). `seedLayer()` was extended to return it;
`cov` and `rim` are both smoothstepped and therefore *saturated*, so they say **whether** you are
inside, never **how far**, and how-far is what gives the bead a face instead of a flat cutout.

Three more obvious candidates were tried first and **measured degenerate on this shader**:

| candidate | measured (p2 / p25 / p50 / p98) | why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| `alpha` (fractal coverage) | 1.000 / 1.000 / 1.000 / 1.000 | the coarsest fold level covers the whole frame |
| `lum` (fractal brightness) | 0.50 / 1.02 / 1.02 / 1.02 | flat — cannot order anything |
| `ldFront` (front-most recursion level) | 0.000 at every percentile | same cause as `alpha` |

…and all three carry audio, which cannot be let into hue. `gSD` has none of those problems: pure
geometry, defined over the whole frame, continuous, and monotone in the exact quantity the eye is
already reading. Its measured range on the playing frame is **−0.35 … +0.50**, which is what
`CD_GAP = 0.40` was fitted to.

`ldFront`/`mFront` are still computed — `mFront` (the fold's own distance field, centred) supplies a
**bounded ±0.03** hue wiggle so the ground has grain instead of reading as a flat poster. Bounded is
the operative word: it is a fraction of a band, so no pixel can claim a depth it is not at.

## Two treatments

- **`?cdmode=0` SHELF** — the bead is a flat plate. Interior red, contour **green**, ground violet.
  Maximum figure/ground separation; the beads sit in front of the lattice.
- **`?cdmode=1` DOME** — the bead is a spherical cap, red at the centre falling to orange, with a
  **hot red fresnel** contour. The silhouette of a round thing is the part of it nearest your eye,
  so painting that red throws the edge at the viewer (`advanced-shader-techniques` §3, "make it
  pop", (b)). Measured: the dome puts ~2× as many pixels in the near band as the shelf.

## The beat pop — where the two doctrines disagree

The ChromaDepth guide says shift hue toward red on a beat so the frame pops forward. Read literally
that is a **global per-beat colour move**, which this shader family has rejected four separate
times (`lattice-vj/HANDOFF.md` §2).

Resolved by **masking the pop to the bead**: the beads punch toward you and the lattice holds still.
That is spatially structured, so it cannot strobe the frame, and it is a better effect than the
global version. Driven by `onsetEnvelope(0.012, 0.16)`, not `beat` — measured per-frame jitter
0.0087 vs 0.3072 for `energyZScore`, and `beat` clocks ~280 BPM against a 125 BPM set. Needs
`?onset_refractory_ms=380`.

Subtracted from depth and **clamped, never wrapped**, so the nearest things saturate at red instead
of rolling over into violet and inverting the 3D. Verified to fire and decay:

```
at rest             mean depth 0.5051
full onset (12 ms)  mean depth 0.4850
300 ms later        mean depth 0.5013
```

## Measurement

`scripts/chromadepth-measure.mjs`. `?cddebug=1` renders the shader's own depth field as greyscale,
so the *rendered hue* can be checked against it after `softClip`, the frame trail, the vignette and
8-bit quantisation have all had their turn. 512×512, all 11 mon, both modes, ~262k px each.

| | SHELF | DOME |
|---|---|---|
| Spearman ρ(depth, hue) | **0.9941** | **0.9956** |
| Pearson r | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| mean \|hue − depth·0.75 − seed2·0.03\| | 0.0009 | 0.0010 |
| near-white fraction (HSL sat < 0.2) | **0.000%** | **0.000%** |
| band mean hue — bead / rim / lattice / ground | 0.034 / 0.324 / 0.531 / 0.711 | 0.034 / 0.313 / 0.542 / 0.711 |

The same frame at `?cdoff=1` (4.frag's palette) measures **0.384%** near-white.

White is unreachable **by construction**, not by tuning: lightness is capped at 0.55 and saturation
floored near 0.92, so the largest channel `hsl2rgb` can emit is `l + s − l·s = 0.964`.

Contact sheet: `journals/lab/shots/chromadepth-5-mon.png` — all 11 mon in both treatments, plus
three depth-field renders and the `cdoff` A/B.

`?cddebug=2` renders the raw depth *inputs* (R = `gSD` centred, G = `mFront`, B = `gCov`); that is
what the "three candidates failed" table above was read off.

## Levers

URL param **and** knob — these four use `max()`, so **0 is real and reachable**, which is the
`LVK()` trap 4.md caught with `?legible=0`:

| lever | knob | default | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| `?cdoff` | `knob_181` | 0 | 1 = chromadepth off, 4.frag exactly. A **switch**, not a fader — a partial crossfade is a blend of two hues at one depth |
| `?cdmode` | `knob_184` | 0 | 0 = shelf, 1 = dome + hot fresnel |
| `?cdpop` | `knob_182` | **0** | how far an onset throws the bead forward. Off by default (the conservative reading of the channel hierarchy); every preset passes 0.5 |
| `?cddebug` | — | 0 | 1 = depth field, 2 = raw depth inputs |

Knob-only tuning constants, house `LVK()` convention (knob 0 = use the baked value):

| lever | knob | baked | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| exposure gain | `knob_183` | 1.90 | how bright the collapsed scalar drives lightness |
| dome falloff | `knob_185` | 4.0 | how fast the dome's face turns away |
| ground gap | `knob_186` | 0.40 | how far out, in cell radii, the ground has fully receded to violet |
| ground grain | `knob_187` | 0.030 | bounded lattice texture in the far band |

## Requirements, and what is deliberately absent

`knob_168 = 1.0` (the bead grid) is a **hard requirement**: with it off there is no figure for the
near band to be, and the frame goes flat violet. The rest of the recognition recipe —
`knob_169=0.60`, `navZoom=0.14`, `legible=1` — is equally load-bearing, for the same reason it was
in 4.frag: ChromaDepth needs a figure to put in front of a ground.

`theme=` and `paletteShift=` are **absent from every preset**. Both rotate `lush()`'s hue, and here
hue belongs to depth alone — a palette preset would be a preset for breaking the 3D. They still run
and still shape the *light*; they just never reach hue. Same for `regionHue`, `melodyFlow`,
`bassNoteFlow`, `pitchClassMean` and the `sectionMode` glide.

`seed2` gets exactly one bounded hue offset, **0.03 max**. The guide suggests using it for
per-device uniqueness, and it can, but it must be small: at the guide's implied 0.07, this device's
`seed2 = 0.755` produced a 0.053 offset — most of the red band — and the measured bead-band hue came
back at **0.097**, i.e. a bead that should read as nearest was painted orange. At 0.03 every
device's near band stays inside red.

## Open

- **Not confirmed through actual glasses.** Everything above is measurable and was measured; that
  the pop *lands* is the one claim only a pair of eyes and a pair of lenses can settle.
- The lattice band (cyan→blue) is thin in frame — the gap goes green→violet quickly. That is
  arguably correct for a three-plane scene, but if the lattice should read as its own layer,
  `CD_GAP` up and `CD_LAT_F` down is the knob pair to try.
- With `autofly=1` the camera drifts through zoom levels, so the bead's apparent size changes and
  with it how much of the frame is in the near band. Same open question 4.md left: whether autofly
  should be range-limited when the figure/ground read matters.
